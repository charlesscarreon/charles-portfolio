## 1. Light mode

- **Why:** the whole site was built and reviewed with the dark theme on, so the light theme was neglected and looks somewhat dim.
- **Already known:** the light theme exists (`data-theme="light"`, kept as a standing constraint, with a contrast fix from the accessibility audit). The newest work (quote cards, internal pages, the form, the achievements, skills and experience cards) was only reviewed in dark.
- **Task 1: Rebalance the light theme's background and accent colour** (planned 2026-11-23, plan only; colours decided 2026-11-23, not yet applied to the site).
  - **Problem (Charles, from a dark and a light screenshot of the homepage):** in the dark theme the near-black background makes the bright teal accent pop and gives most highlights a glow. In the light theme the very bright background with the dimmer teal makes the accent dull, and the background is unusually bright and can strain the eyes. Either the background, the accent colour or both should change.
  - **Today:** dark `--bg #0f1115`, `--accent #2dd4a7`, `--accent-strong #5eead4`. Light `--bg #f7f8fa`, `--surface #ffffff` (cards), `--surface-2 #f0f2f5`, `--border #dde1e7`, `--text-strong #000000`, `--accent #0e7a70`, `--accent-strong #0f766e`, `--link #0e7a70`. The accent is also used for body-size link text, so it must keep enough contrast on every light surface (the standing accessibility target is WCAG AA).
  - **Options to choose from (undecided):** (a) change only the background: a softer, slightly tinted off-white or warm grey instead of near-white, cards a little lighter or darker than the page so they still separate; (b) change only the accent: a more saturated, more vivid teal or a different accent for light that reads brighter, as long as text and links stay readable; (c) both, with a separate light palette designed together instead of reusing the dark one's ideas. Highlight effects also need a light-theme version, since a glow does not read on a bright page: for example a stronger tinted border, a coloured shadow or a tinted fill.
  - **What it touches:** the `:root` and `[data-theme="light"]` variables at the top of `styles.css`; the hard-coded copies of the colours (`#0e7a70` and `#f7f8fa` also appear in `api/_lib/emailTemplate.js`, `#f7f8fa` in the theme-colour meta tag set by `script.js` and the pages' `<meta name="theme-color">`, `#2dd4a7` in `favicon.svg` and in some `rgba(45, 212, 167, ...)` glows in `styles.css`); the many `color-mix` rules that mix the accent (about 70); the skill-level colours; the internal pages and the emails (which copy the website's light look, so they follow whatever is chosen).
  - **To decide first:** which of (a), (b) or (c); then a short palette try-out (Charles looks at it in the browser) before the whole site is changed.
  - **Palette try-out (2026-11-23, `dev\preview-light.bat`, port 8128):** Charles looked at 11 backgrounds and 18 accents (separately, plus shuffle). He chose the softer-background direction (a cooler, less bright page instead of near-white) and the teal needed a real change of shade, not a small one. Shortlist: **Warm paper + Pine** (his favourite), **Soft teal tint + Slate ink**, **Sage + Slate ink**, and **Sand** as a deeper alternative to Warm paper. Values in the tool: Warm paper `--bg #f2efe8`, `--surface #fbfaf6`, `--surface-2 #e9e5db`, `--border #d6d0c2`, text `#22211d`, muted `#5f5b50`; Sand `#efe9dd`, `#faf7f0`, `#e4dccb`, `#d0c6b0`, text `#26221a`, muted `#625b4a`; Soft teal tint `#e9f1f0`, `#f6faf9`, `#dbe8e6`, `#c3d5d2`; Sage `#e5ebe3`, `#f3f6f1`, `#d7e0d4`, `#bfcbbb`; Pine accent and link `#134e4a`, hover `#0b3633`; Slate ink accent `#334155`, link `#1e293b`, hover `#0f172a`. Not final: the final pick is still to be made, and the hard-coded teal glows and `color-mix` rules did not follow the accent in the preview.
  - **Open points for the final pick:** Pine and Slate ink are dark accents, so the "pop" in light comes from contrast and not brightness; check how the glows, hover states, skill-level colours, hero decoration and the flashlight (Task 3) look with them; decide whether the dark theme keeps its bright teal (so the two themes share an identity only through shape and layout) or the accent is a related green in both.
  - **Decision (Charles, 2026-11-23): Paper deeper + Pine bright.** Light theme values: `--bg #ebe7de`, `--surface #f8f6f1`, `--surface-2 #e0dbd0`, `--border #cdc7ba`, `--text #22211d`, `--muted #5f5b50`, `--shadow 0 4px 20px rgba(20, 30, 40, 0.11)`, `--accent #17665f`, `--accent-strong #135a54`, `--link #135a54`, `--hover #0e4640`; `--accent-contrast` stays white (measured: white on the accent 6.8:1, link text on the page 6.5:1, muted text 5.5:1, body text 13.1:1). Runner-up, recorded as an option: **Warm paper + Pine** (`--bg #f2efe8`, `--surface #fbfaf6`, `--surface-2 #e9e5db`, `--border #d6d0c2`, `--text #22211d`, `--muted #5f5b50`; accent `#134e4a`, hover `#0b3633`). The dark theme is not changed by this decision.
  - **When it is built:** replace the light variables in `styles.css`; update the hard-coded copies (`api/_lib/emailTemplate.js` teal `#0e7a70` and page colour, the `theme-color` in `script.js`), the `rgba(45, 212, 167, ...)` glows and the `color-mix` rules that still assume the old light colours, the skill-level colours if they clash, the internal pages and emails; then re-check Task 2 (dimming) and Task 3 (hero objects and flashlight) on the new colours. The logo rework can now start, since the light colours are settled.

- **Task 2: Make the hover dimming of neighbouring cards read as dimmed in the light theme** (planned 2026-11-23, plan only, nothing decided yet).
  - **Problem (Charles, from a dark and a light screenshot of the homepage featured projects):** in the dark theme, hovering a card dims its neighbours so the hovered one stands out. In the light theme the neighbours only look faded or blurred, not dimmed, so the hovered card does not stand out the same way.
  - **Cause:** the dimming is one rule that sets `opacity: 0.65` on the other cards (featured, project, achievements, skills, certificate and recommendation cards, on hover and on keyboard focus, in several places in `styles.css`). Opacity fades a card towards the page colour: on a near-black page that looks darker, on a near-white page it only looks washed out and lower in contrast.
  - **Options (undecided):** (a) a separate, lower opacity for the light theme; (b) for the light theme, darken or desaturate the neighbours instead of fading them (for example a brightness or grayscale filter, or a dark tint over the card, so the images dim too); (c) combine a small fade with a slight grey tint. The same choice should apply to every card type that uses the shared dimming, so the effect stays the same across the site.
  - **To keep:** pointer devices only (and the existing keyboard-focus version), the reduced-motion rule, and separate rules so a browser without `:has()` only loses the dimming. A filter can be costly on large images, so the try-out should check it still feels smooth.
  - **Related:** it depends partly on Task 1, because the amount of dimming that looks right depends on the light background and cards chosen there.

- **Task 3: Make the hero's floating objects and pointer "flashlight" visible in the light theme** (planned 2026-11-23, plan only, nothing decided yet; partly helped by Task 1).
  - **Problem (Charles, from a light-theme screenshot of the hero):** in the dark theme the floating objects have a faint, not solid, opacity but still pop because of the contrast with the dark background. In the light theme they just blend into the background. In the dark theme, hovering the hero shows a "flashlight" around the pointer that reveals the hidden circuitry; in the light theme this does not work properly because the light colour of the effect blends with the background.
  - **Today:** the floating objects (`.deco` in `styles.css`, placed and drifting by `initHeroDecor` in `script.js`) are thin 1.5px teal strokes at about 16% opacity (`--o`), in the accent colour. The flashlight is `.hero-decor::before` (a soft circle of the accent at about 7%) plus `.hero-circuit` (the circuit traces drawn by `script.js`, at 35% opacity with a pulse at 60%), both shown only inside a circle that follows the pointer through `--mx` and `--my`. All of it uses the one `--accent` colour, so on a near-white page a faint teal on white has too little contrast to read.
  - **Options (undecided):** for the floating objects: a stronger opacity, a thicker stroke or a darker, more saturated colour for the light theme only. For the flashlight, which is a "light" effect and does not exist on a bright page: (a) turn it into a "darkening" or inking effect for light (the traces drawn in a darker teal or ink colour, the circle a slightly darker tint instead of a lighter glow); (b) keep a glow but give it a stronger, more saturated colour with a soft shadow; (c) reveal the circuitry in a clearly darker line colour only. Whatever is chosen should still feel like the dark version's reveal.
  - **To keep:** pointer devices only, no animation for reduced motion, the no-JS look unchanged (the objects are simply there), and the existing performance work on the pointer tracking.
  - **Related:** Task 1 (a new light background or accent changes how visible these are, so decide that first and then judge what is still missing). The decorative objects and circuitry are on the hero of the other pages too, so they follow the same fix.

- **Tasks:** more to be added.

  - **Cause:** the hero contact row (`.hero .contact` in `styles.css`, markup in `homepage.template.html`) is one wrapping flex row holding the email link and the three icons. ## 3. Recommendations backend

- **Why:** implement properly all the backend features the recommendations need.
- **Already known (from `dev/CONTEXT.md`):** the flow works on a Vercel preview (submit, writer confirmation, owner decision, public list, admin with hide, delete and featured top three). Still to do: the daily cron job with the 24-hour same-thread reminder email and the weekly digest email (both to get the same look as the other emails and a preview in `dev\preview-email.bat`); the owner-only JSON export and the repo snapshot fallback; the rate limits beyond the basic throttle (daily and site-wide limits, the signed timestamp check); the full no-JS and no-CSS test pass (`?css=0`, `?js=0`, both, neither, plus lynx or w3m, slow 3G and reader mode); the accessibility check.
- **Tasks** (planned 2026-11-23, plan only, one branch for the rework):
  1. **Daily cron job.** One Vercel cron route, protected by the cron secret, that runs the other scheduled jobs and the 48-hour cleanup of unconfirmed entries. Tasks 2 and 3 depend on it.
     - **Decided 2026-11-25: folded into Task 2.** The 48-hour cleanup is already done by Redis (unconfirmed entries are saved with a 48-hour expiry, and confirming removes it), so a cron with no jobs would do nothing. The route (`api/cron.js`, daily at 23:00 UTC since the digest was added (first 01:00), protected by `CRON_SECRET`) is built together with the reminder.
  2. **24-hour reminder email.** Built 2026-11-25 (`api/_lib/reminders.js`, `reminderSentAt` on the entry, a fresh decision link, at most 20 per run; outside production `/api/cron?minAgeHours=0` skips the wait); not yet tested on a preview or in Gmail. Sent when a confirmed entry is still undecided 24 hours after the first email, as a reply in the same Gmail thread (read the real Message-ID back from Resend, and test in a real Gmail inbox). Undecided entries stay pending with no expiry. Uses the shared email template, with a preview in `dev\preview-email.bat`.
  3. **Weekly digest email.** Lists all pending entries, including ones under the 24-hour limit. Same template and preview. Built 2026-11-25 (`api/_lib/digest.js`, `buildDigestEmail` in `api/_lib/ownerMail.js`, a list block in the shared template, two previews). Decided: sent Monday around 7 AM Manila time, so the daily cron moved to 23:00 UTC (`0 23 * * *`) and the digest goes out on the Sunday UTC run; reminders now also go out around 7 AM. Lists every awaiting entry (up to 15, with a count of the rest), each with a fresh decision link; sends nothing when none is waiting. Outside production `/api/cron?digest=1` sends it on any day. Not yet tested.
  4. **Owner-only JSON export.** A download of the published testimonies behind the admin login, also usable to restore or move the data. Built 2026-11-25 (`api/_lib/backup.js`, a "Download backup" button at the bottom of `/admin`, a POST action `export` behind the admin login and the same-origin check). Decided: a full backup, all four states, with email addresses and private profile links, so it is for the owner only; unconfirmed entries are left out; fields are copied from an allow-list (never token hashes, the IP hash or email-service ids). File: `recommendations-backup-<date>.json` with `format`, `version`, `exportedAt`, `environment`, `counts`, `featured` and `entries` by state. An import is not built. Not yet tested.
  5. **Repo snapshot fallback.** `/recommendations` falls back to a snapshot file in the repo when the database is down. Built 2026-11-25. Decided: refreshed by hand from the downloaded backup (no automatic commits, no GitHub token on the server). The snapshot is `api/_lib/recommendations-snapshot.json` (beside the code so Vercel bundles it), read by `api/_lib/snapshot.js`; `/recommendations` and the homepage use it only when the database read fails, cached 10 seconds, with a log line in Vercel. It holds only public fields (never emails, private links or tokens). Refresh with `dev\make-snapshot.bat "path\to\recommendations-backup-DATE.json"` (warns when the backup came from a non-production environment). An empty or missing snapshot behaves as before (the unavailable message). **To do by Charles:** after the site is live with real recommendations, download a production backup, run the script and commit the file; the committed file is empty for now.
